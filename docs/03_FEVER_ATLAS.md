# MISERIA AETERNA: The Fever Atlas

**Status: PROPOSED.** A palette of concepts for the weird of Cinerea, under the God's ruling D-034 and the Fever system (P-030 to P-033). Each concept is a phenomenon the simulation can produce: it has a driver in simulation state, a root in Cinerea's lore, and a second look that reveals it. None is canon until the God accepts it; all are meant to be built from, combined, and cut.

**How this was made.** Four seers each invented ten concepts through one lens (the land and sky; the body and the dead; the life of the living; the Sight and the Books). A judge kept 25, merged near-duplicates, rejected cliches and anything that would give mortals proof of God, and named the gaps. The First Light then wrote 18 more to fill those gaps (marked ✦): Cinerea's own congenital sickness, the sky at world scale, daylight dreamcore, joy and labor, the cities and the climb toward Advanced Gothic, deliberate cruelty, the wrong rite, the Light's visits, the Shore seen from inside, mountains, and the Strepitus.

---

## The Laws of the Fever

These recur in every strong concept. They are the craft rules for making more.

1. **Mutate a real thing; never insert a strange one.** Every concept begins from something true: cropmarks, gossamer, sun dogs, children playing at funerals, weathered statues, cockled vellum, apprentices missetting type. The weird is a small deviation inside a real fact, so it disappears into grim realism.
2. **The second look is a count, a bearing, or a rhythm.** The hollows equal the number of the unmourned; the crow rings close toward one house; the barley swells at the pace of a sleeper. The uncanny is a correlation with simulation state that only patient watching reveals.
3. **Each phenomenon reads out one variable** (grief, remembrance, despair, faith, rite correctness, sharing, joy) and has an escalation and a release that only mortals can perform.
4. **Despair gives horror a direction and a debtor.** Scary things move, and they move toward the household that owes the grief or made the trade.
5. **The uncanny speaks in the mortal's own idiom:** her handwriting, his vowels, a mother's first sung line, the rememberer's broken nose on a saint.
6. **Dreamcore distorts the physics of ordinary things, not their inventory.** A room longer inside than outside, October light at a July angle, a noon nailed over one field. No new objects; wrong scale, time, and light.
7. **Take sense away rather than add it.** Silence, delay, stillness, sound going underwater. Never a jump scare.
8. **Only the God sees the whole.** Each mortal gets a fragment they can explain away; the full pattern is legible only from above or in the Books. The player is a lonely witness.
9. **The four rites are the grammar of the weird.** Ash-fields breathe, crows refuse the body, lamps come home, the ground keeps hollows. The weird takes each Kindred's form.
10. **The strange peace is earned, and it is just as uncanny as the horror.** Animals are its witnesses: dogs sleep through warm rain, larks sleep at noon, a fox sleeps on the rose path.
11. **Color is the anomaly marker.** Coral ember where it cannot be (underwater, in dewdrops, as a seam across frost); blood-rose only from above or in the darkest hour; never white.
12. **The God's own instruments catch the fever.** The Books sweat, file infants under lullabies, copy themselves. The player cannot stand outside the world's weirdness.
13. **✦ Every phenomenon has a twin in the other tone.** A held light can be bought by a Bargain (The Bought Noon) or given by joy (The White Afternoon). A lamp can come home in horror (The Lamps Coming Home) or a candle lean toward a rightly mourned bed (The Candles Lean Toward the Bed). The second set of footprints can walk beside you or lead you off the cliff. Design every weird thing with its twin, and let the simulation choose which one appears.

## I. The Strangely Relaxing

*The fever's sweet delirium: high Heat, calm Tone.* (14 concepts)

### The Ember's Wake

*Lens: the land and sky.* *Absorbs: The Ancestry of Flame.*

After a fire is carried from an old hearth to a new one, the road it travelled stays warm for days. Down the middle of the track, frost will not form on a stripe as wide as one walking person; the puddles along it steam gently while the ditches on either side are skinned with ice, and moths turn slow circles above nothing. At night it shows as a hair-thin coral seam stitched across the dark land between two roofs, bending exactly where the carrier stopped to shield the pot from the wind, and once, beside a grave, where they stopped to say a name.

- **Driver:** A hearth-transfer event lays the wake along the carrier's actual route, with the source hearth's age setting how warm it is and how long it lasts, and it fades as the new hearth grows into its own.
- **Root:** Fire carried from old hearths to new ones, and the Lights of the Living strung across the night map.
- **Second look:** It looks like a lane where the sun happened to fall, until you see that the thaw follows one person's footsteps exactly, detours and all, at night in the dead of winter.

### The Field That Keeps Her Breath

*Lens: the land and sky.*

In an Ash-Giver barley field where the ashes of someone well mourned were scattered, the grain moves in long slow swells on windless evenings, rising and settling at the pace of a person asleep. The swells always roll toward the cottage of the one who grieved hardest and stop at its hedge the way a tide stops at a sea wall. The stalks brush together with a soft hush, hush, the sound a mother makes over a cradle, and larks drop into the field at midday to sleep as if it were dusk.

- **Driver:** A correctly performed ash-scattering with deep grief sets the size of the field's swell, its rhythm locks to the chief mourner's sleep, and it goes still for good on the night that mourner dies.
- **Root:** Ash-Giver funerals that burn the dead and scatter them on the fields, and the law that grief and the right rite lay the dead to rest.
- **Second look:** Grain rippling in a field is the most ordinary sight in the world, but this grain moves in still air, toward one house, at the pace of sleep.

### Cold Ash on the Fevered

*Lens: the body and the dead.*

Past midnight in a smoke-black hovel, a mother dozes upright on a stool beside her burning child, and a pale ash begins to fall from the rafters above the bed, slow and thick as the first snow of the year. Each flake glows a faint coral as it drifts down and turns gray the moment it touches the sweating skin, with a tiny hiss like a spark dropped on wet stone, until the room is full of a soft sound like rain on thatch. By dawn the blanket is silvered with ash, the child breathes long and slow, and the forehead under the mother's palm is cool. Not one flake has landed on her.

- **Driver:** A fever crisis that breaks into recovery while a caregiver keeps nursing at the bedside through the whole night, with the ashfall growing thicker the higher the fever peaked.
- **Root:** Cinerea is the Ashen, and here the world's own ash does for the sick what the Ash-Givers believe their scattered dead do for the fields: ash that was once fire draws the fire out of the living.
- **Second look:** Soot sifting down from old thatch in a smoky hut is ordinary, but a second look shows it falling only inside the outline of the bed, hissing where it lands, and never touching the person keeping watch.

### One Breath of the Hungry Village

*Lens: the body and the dead.*

Deep in a famine winter the village sleeps under hard frost, and every chimney and cracked doorway lets out a plume of white breath at the same moment, as if the whole hamlet were one body lying on its back in the snow. Between breaths there is no sound at all; then comes the exhale, one long sigh through all the thatch together, and every hearth-glow swells a little and sinks again like a chest under a blanket. As the night goes on the breaths slow to one every long minute, then longer, and the stars between them are very sharp. Only one roof at the edge of the village puffs out of time, quick and shallow, like a sick dog panting.

- **Driver:** Food per head below the famine threshold in a settlement where households still share their stores and carry fire to cold hearths; every household that shares and keeps its hearth lit joins the rhythm, while a hoarding household or one whose hearth has gone out breathes alone and out of step.
- **Root:** It grows from the Lights of the Living and the custom of carrying fire from old hearths to new ones: a village that keeps one fire across many hearths sleeps as one sleeper.
- **Second look:** Smoke and breath-fog on a frozen night are as ordinary as anything gets, until the eye notices forty chimneys rising and falling together and slowing together, like a sleeper sinking deeper.

### The Candles Lean Toward the Bed

*Lens: the body and the dead.* *Absorbs: The Vigil Goes Under the Water.*

Around the bed of someone dying among their own people, every candle flame in the room slowly bends toward the pillow and stays bent, like river reeds combed by a gentle current, though no draft moves the shutters. The dogs outside, the cart wheels and the wind in the eaves all fade away, and in their place comes the slow lap of dark water and, very far off, the creak of an oarlock. In the washbasin beside the bed the water has gone deep and black, and on its surface lies a distant shoreline strung with thousands of small lanterns and one empty hook. When the last breath leaves, a new lantern kindles on that hook with the exact flicker of the bedside candle, and every flame in the room stands straight again.

- **Driver:** A dying mortal with kin keeping vigil in the room and their Kindred's rite already prepared (the shroud sewn, the pyre stacked, the raft woven, or the hill path swept), so that both grief and rite are certain before death arrives.
- **Root:** The Shore of lanterns reaches back into the room for a death that will be rightly mourned, the moment a soul passes from the Lights of the Living to the lights of remembrance.
- **Second look:** Candlelight wavering in a sickroom is unremarkable, and from where the mourners sit the basin reflects only the rafters; the Shore in the water can be seen only from the God's raised angle.

### The Rose Path

*Lens: the life of the living.*

In the deepest hour of a moonless night, seen from above, the village has a second road: a faint blood-rose line no wider than a footprint, worn into the frost along the exact way one plain young woman walks every day, to the well, to the widow's door, to the sick boy's shutter, to the ferry and back. Wherever she stopped to help someone, the line pools into a soft rosette, like wax gathered at the foot of a candle. A fox crosses it, slows, lies down on it, and sleeps. By first light it is only wet ground, and the woman is drawing water, coughing, ordinary.

- **Driver:** A Sent soul's daily route and the number of kind acts done along it build up over the seasons and are drawn only during the darkest hour of moonless nights.
- **Root:** It grows from the Sent, angels born as ordinary humans, and from Cinerea's holy light, which is a blood-rose ramp and never white.
- **Second look:** It passes for moonlight on a muddy footpath until you see that there is no moon, the light is rose, and the path ends at every door where someone was helped and never at the tavern.

### Gossamer of the Unanswered

*Lens: the Sight and the Books.*

At dawn after a long night of prayer, the ash-scattered stubble fields of an Ash-Giver village are roofed in gossamer, thousands of fine threads running from every chimney down to the furrows and sagging under dew. Each bead of dew holds a pinprick of coral where it should hold the gray sky. The wind has stopped dead; a woman walks out to the well, the threads part around her shins without breaking, and the only sounds are a thrush and the creak of the well rope. In the Book of Prayers, the margins of that village's page are stitched through with the same fine thread, one stitch for every night someone prayed, was not answered, and prayed again.

- **Driver:** Forms when a village's prayer threads go unanswered for many nights in a row while its faith stays high and its despair stays low, and the density of the gossamer scales with the number of persistent, repeated prayers.
- **Root:** Grows from Prayers Rising as threads of light, the Hidden God's silence, and faith as an act of will: prayers that are never answered but never abandoned settle back onto the land instead of fading.
- **Second look:** Autumn gossamer from ballooning spiders is a real, ordinary sight over stubble fields, but a second look shows every strand runs from a chimney and every dewdrop glows coral under a colorless sky.

### The Valley Hums Her Lullaby

*Lens: the Sight and the Books.*

From high above at night, the village's noises lose their edges: a dog, a cough, a door bar dropping, a cart wheel on frozen ruts, and forty hearths settling into embers all melt into one low sound, like your own blood heard through a pillow. Slowly the sound finds a tune, and the tune is the lullaby the oldest mother in the village is singing right now, with its long fall at the end of the third line. When she drifts off mid-verse, the whole valley slips down one step and keeps humming without her, softer, while below it the Lights of the Living glow steady, forty coral pinpricks rising and falling like sleeping chests.

- **Driver:** Plays only when the camera is high above a village at night, every household hearth is lit, and all of that year's dead received their rites; the melody comes from the lullabies the sim records being sung in its houses that night and shifts key when the singer it is drawn from falls asleep.
- **Root:** Grows from the Lights of the Living, the hearth fires carried from old homes to new, and the lullabies each Kindred hands down: a village at peace with its dead sounds like a mother singing it to sleep.
- **Second look:** It begins as ordinary distant night ambience, but within a minute the listener realizes the murmur has a melody, that the melody belongs to one living woman, and that it changes when she stops.

### The White Afternoon ✦

*Lens: the life of the living.*

After a wedding in a half-roofed chapel, the afternoon will not end. The sun hangs at the third hour for longer than an hour, the light goes creamy and overexposed, and the shadows of the guests lie still on the grass while the guests themselves dance. The fiddler's tune folds back on itself so gently no one notices it has played eleven times. Children's laughter arrives a little after their mouths open. When the light finally moves, every guest says it was the longest and best day of their life, and none of them can say how long it was.

- **Driver:** A celebration whose joy across the gathered souls is very high, in a place where the world's Heat is also high, holds the hour; the hold lasts longer the more of the guests had been grieving before they came.
- **Root:** The fever of Cinerea in its calm tone; the twin of The Bought Noon: one held light is bought by a Bargain, the other is given by joy.
- **Second look:** A long summer afternoon at a country wedding is the most ordinary happiness there is, until you notice the shadows have not moved while the dancers have.

### The Noon Sleep ✦

*Lens: the land and sky.*

At the hottest noon of harvest, the whole valley lies down at once: the reapers in the stubble, the dogs in the shade of the cart, the miller on his sacks, the cows in the river shallows, even the crows, which drop out of the air onto the furrows and fold their wings. The light is white-gold and shadowless. Nothing moves but the mill wheel, turning with no one to watch it, and the only sound is a single grasshopper. Then, between one breath and the next, everyone wakes together and goes back to work, and no one knows they all slept.

- **Driver:** Harvest noon in a valley whose Tone is calm, whose people are fed, and whose Heat is high; every soul with a filled belly and a light heart joins the sleep, and anyone in despair stays awake alone in the stilled field.
- **Root:** The fever's sweet delirium, and the dignity of labor in a world where most labor is grief.
- **Second look:** Harvesters napping at noon is the oldest painting in the world, but here the birds fell asleep in flight, and one man stands awake among the sleepers, looking around.

### Ash-Bloom ✦

*Lens: the land and sky.*

The second spring after a village burns, the gray ash-fields where it stood come up in pale morels, thousands of them, and they grow in lines: the outlines of walls, the square of a hearth, the narrow strip of a doorway, the whole lost village redrawn in fungus on the ash like a builder's plan. Children come out with baskets and pick them room by room without being told which room they are in. The morels taste of smoke and bread. By midsummer they are gone, and the ash is green.

- **Driver:** The ash harvest after a burning, where the burned village's dead were mourned; the bloom traces every building whose last household was laid to rest, and leaves blank the ones whose dead were not.
- **Root:** Cinerea's rot and ash as the soil of the next spring; the Ash harvest; the Earth-Keepers' houses remembered in the shape of their floors.
- **Second look:** Morels after a fire are real and ordinary, but these grow in straight lines, in the plan of the houses, and one rectangle in the middle stays bare.

### The Well That Goes Down to the Shore ✦

*Lens: the body and the dead.*

On summer evenings the children of the First Valley drop pebbles into the old well by the churchyard and count, and the count is always longer than any well could be deep. When the stone finally lands it lands in water far below, and after it comes, faint as breath, the lap of still water and the creak of an oarlock. The old women say the well goes down to where the dead sleep. The children are not afraid. They lean over the rim, call the names of their grandmothers down into the dark, and wait, smiling, for the echo to come back softer.

- **Driver:** A well within sight of a churchyard; the depth of the count and the clarity of the water sounds rise with the number of that churchyard's dead who are fully at rest.
- **Root:** The Shore, the Earth-Keepers' graveyards, and remembrance spoken aloud as something that keeps a lantern bright.
- **Second look:** Children dropping stones down a well is a picture of every childhood, until the count reaches thirty and the stone has still not landed.

### The Flame That Does Not Lean ✦

*Lens: the life of the living.*

On the night after a person comes through a great trial with their will tempered, the first fire they light, a candle, a hearth, a forge, burns perfectly still. The shutters bang in the wind, the thatch hisses, the other candles in the house flatten and gutter, and this one flame stands straight as a nail, its coral body clear to the wick. Moths circle it and are not burned. A smith's apprentice will tell his grandchildren that the iron he worked by that light was the best of his life.

- **Driver:** A Trial of Will that ends Tempered; the stillness lasts one night and its clarity scales with the weight of the blow that was survived.
- **Root:** Faith and will as a forge; the carried hearth; the coral ember that is the color of human life.
- **Second look:** A steady candle on a calm night is nothing, but this is not a calm night, and only this flame is calm.

### The View From the Lantern ✦

*Lens: the Sight and the Books.*

On the Shore, the God can look from inside one lantern back toward the land. The view is the world as that dead person remembers it: their village smaller than it is, too gold, overexposed like a summer afternoon from childhood, and the people they loved standing still in the places where they last saw them, mid-gesture, a hand raised, a basket on a hip. As they are forgotten the picture fades at its edges like old paper. When someone on the living side speaks their name, the colors come back for a breath, and the raised hand finishes its wave.

- **Driver:** A lantern's view is built from the dead soul's last memories of places and people; its brightness and the width of its picture follow how strongly the living remember them, and a spoken name refreshes it for a moment.
- **Root:** The Shore, remembrance as brightness, and dreamcore's overexposed memory at the wrong scale.
- **Second look:** It is only a memory of a village, until you see that the waving hand completes its gesture exactly when a girl in the real village says her grandfather's name.

## II. The Subtly Uncomfortable

*Something is off, and it is beautiful, and you cannot stop looking.* (17 concepts)

### The Bought Noon

*Lens: the land and sky.*

Over a field whose farmer accepted the Bargain for a good harvest, it is always a little after noon. The sky above that one plot is a hard, clean, too-blue enamel with a flat brass sun nailed into it, and the wheat beneath is so gold it looks lacquered, every stalk's shadow a small black coin at its foot, while all around it the valley has gone to slate dusk and the village lamps are lit. There are no birds and no insects, only a dry, endless ticking, which is the grain splitting its husks, and the farmer stands at the edge in the dark, looking in, and does not step into the light.

- **Driver:** An Unlit's Bargain for land or harvest holds the noon for as long as the Bargain holds, and each season its square edge creeps toward whatever he loved and traded away, such as his wife's herb bed or his son's grave.
- **Root:** The Bargain, which offered this man what he wanted in exchange for what he loved, shown as SOMNIUM's too-blue sky over ordinary farmland.
- **Second look:** From above it reads as a lucky farm caught in a break of sun, until you notice that the light never moves through the day, the shadows never lengthen, and the edge of the brightness runs ruler-straight along the property line.

### The Autumn That Stayed

*Lens: the land and sky.*

In a valley where one plague autumn killed more people than could be mourned, that autumn never quite leaves. On a hot midsummer morning a low fog rolls up off the river smelling of wet leaves and wood smoke, and the oaks, still fully green, drop red and brown leaves into it that crackle under the cart wheels and are gone by noon. Swallows line the eaves in rows as if about to migrate and never go, and inside the fog the light is the low copper light of October, slanting in at the angle of a different month.

- **Driver:** While the unmourned dead of a mass-death event stay above a threshold, the fog returns every year in the anniversary weeks; it spreads into other seasons as remembrance fades and despair rises, and it shrinks as the overdue rites are finally performed.
- **Root:** Unmourned death pooling as Tenebrae, with TESTIMONIUM's replay of the land's memory spilling over into weather and season.
- **Second look:** River fog and falling leaves are the most common things in Cinerea, but not in high summer, not from green trees and not under a sun at the wrong height.

### The Bed Made for No One

*Lens: the body and the dead.*

Every morning, before anyone in the house wakes, the dead man's sleeping place is found made with a care nobody in this family has ever had: the blanket pulled drum-tight, the corners folded square, drawn up over a long hollow exactly his length. The pillow keeps a dent, and the dent is warm. The family eats its gray porridge a step away and edges around the bed to reach the door, and no one pulls the blanket back, though if you watch long enough it rises and falls very slightly. Each day the hollow is a finger's width longer than the day before.

- **Driver:** A household member who died without grief or rite (away from home, in a pit, or into a family too broken to mourn), tracked as the household's grief deficit: a late mourning or rite leaves the blanket thrown back and the bed cold, while rising despair lengthens the hollow until one night the bed is empty, the door stands open, and an Unmourned walks.
- **Root:** This is the household-sized seed of the Unmourned, a grief taking on a body in the one place the dead man still has a claim to, his own bed.
- **Second look:** A neat bed in a poor house reads as plain housekeeping, until one notices that every other bed in the room is a tangle of rags and that this one is breathing.

### The Room That Grows in Labor

*Lens: the body and the dead.*

As a hard labor stretches into its second night, the birthing room in the cottage's cutaway starts to be larger inside than the cottage is outside: the scrubbed floorboards run on and on away from the door, and the bed stands far off at the end beneath a little window holding a sky bluer than Cinerea's sky ever is. The midwife walks the length of the room with a basin of hot water, and keeps walking, and by the time she reaches the bed the water has stopped steaming. The mother's cries arrive late, after her mouth has already closed, like thunder after lightning. If mother and child both live, the room is back to its true size from one moment to the next, and nobody speaks of the walk.

- **Driver:** A childbirth that runs well past normal length with high risk to the mother, the room stretching further as her danger grows; if the mother dies, the room stays long and the window stays blue until she is mourned.
- **Root:** The mother is standing halfway onto the Shore, and the space between the Lights of the Living and the lanterns of the dead shows up as distance inside a single room, in SOMNIUM's register of wrong scale.
- **Second look:** At the diorama's small scale an interior cutaway looks ordinary, and only careful watching catches the inner walls running past the outer ones, a walk that takes too many steps, and a cry that comes after the mouth has closed.

### The Last Face of the Saint

*Lens: the life of the living.*

At a crossroads shrine the stone saint has been rained on for three hundred years, and her face has worn down into something new: a heavy jaw, a nose broken once and set crooked, a small mole high on the left cheek. Every dawn an old woman climbs the hill with a rag and a stub of candle and wipes that face clean, and it is her own face she is wiping, shaped by her own hands over forty years of mornings. Nobody else in the valley still knows the saint's name. Lichen has crept up the plinth in the pattern of a lifted hem, as if the statue is about to step down and walk home behind her.

- **Driver:** When the number of living mortals who remember a saint falls to one, the statue's sprite slowly blends toward that mortal's features, because their memory is now the only thing keeping the saint's Shore lantern lit.
- **Root:** It grows from remembrance as literal brightness on the Shore: a half-forgotten saint is a lantern kept burning by one living memory.
- **Second look:** Weathered statues lose their features all the time, so the face reads as ordinary erosion until you put the old woman beside it and see the same broken nose.

### The Children Play Funerals

*Lens: the life of the living.*

On the feast day, while the adults dance in the square under strings of coral lamps, five children crouch in the gutter behind the tannery and play at rites. They fold a boat from bark, stand a stub of tallow on it, and send it down the drain water while they sing the River-Senders' lament over a beggar's name that no grown person in town has said aloud since the man was tipped into the lime pit last winter. The little flame goes around the corner and does not come back. When their mothers ask where they learned that song, the children say they have always known it, and they are telling the truth.

- **Driver:** An unmourned or wrongly rited death in the settlement within the past year, together with idle children during a festival, triggers a play-rite in the dead person's own Kindred form, and it counts as a small fraction of real mourning.
- **Root:** It grows from the four Kindreds' rites of the dead, from the rule that grief lays the dead to rest and the right rite counts more, and from the unmourned residue that feeds the Tenebrae.
- **Second look:** Children played at funerals in every plague century, so the scene reads as grim realism until you notice they are using the dead man's exact Kindred rite and his name, neither of which they could have known.

### The Honest Echo

*Lens: the life of the living.*

A priest who stopped believing three winters ago still preaches every seventh day, and his voice rolls up into the soot-black vault and comes back down half a breath late. The echo has his voice, his cough and his northern vowels, but not his words: where he says 'He hears us,' the vault answers 'no one is listening,' gently, as if it is sorry for him. The congregation hears only stone, shuffles and coughs, while every candle on the altar leans very slightly away from him, toward the open door and the dark.

- **Driver:** A clergy member's private faith drops below a threshold while their public preaching continues, and the echo's text comes from the gap between the doctrine they speak and the belief they hold, fading again if they keep preaching as a deliberate act of will.
- **Root:** It grows from the silent Void that only mortal will can give a voice, and from faith as an act of will, a forge that starts talking back when it is left cold.
- **Second look:** Every stone chapel has an echo, and to the congregation it is only that; only the priest and the watching God catch that the words are wrong.

### Entered Under a Lullaby

*Lens: the Sight and the Books.*

In a hard winter the Book of the Dead fills with infants who died before their naming day, and because they have no names the Book enters each one under the first line its mother sang to it. One Earth-Keeper parish page holds 'Hush now, the ash is falling' fourteen times, in fourteen different unsteady hands, each line ending in a tiny painted cradle, and the great initial at the top of the page is a cradle hood drawn around nothing. Turning the page makes no rustle, only a woman's voice humming that one line and breaking off, fourteen times, each a little higher than the last.

- **Driver:** Each infant death before a naming rite creates an entry keyed to the lullaby the sim recorded that mother singing, the repeated lines multiply with winter infant mortality in the parish, and the humming plays only while that mother is still alive and grieving.
- **Root:** Grows from the law that grief and the right rite lay the dead to rest and from the Kindreds' lullabies: an unnamed child has grief but no name, so the Book files it under the only words its mother gave it.
- **Second look:** Winter infant deaths and unnamed babies are plain medieval realism, yet a page of one lullaby line repeated in many mothers' handwriting reads like a nursery seen in a fever.

### The Fevered Page

*Lens: the Sight and the Books.*

When the sweating sickness reaches a district, its pages in the Book of the Living run a fever. The vellum flushes pink and damp at the edges, the ink beads up like sweat on an upper lip, and the gold leaf on the initials lifts and blisters like skin under a hot poultice. The names of the sick slide slowly down their ruled lines the way a fevered man slides down his pillow, the whole page gives off a faint dry chattering like teeth under too many blankets, and when the fever breaks for someone, their name dries where it lies, a little crooked, forever.

- **Driver:** Each page's flush, sweating ink, and blistering track the live count and severity of fever cases in the households it records, and a name settles in place the moment the sim marks that person recovered or dead.
- **Root:** Grows from Cinerea being born sick and decaying from its first years: the Book of the Living is a body made of the living, so it catches what they catch.
- **Second look:** Cockled vellum and flaking gilt are normal damage in real old manuscripts, but here the damage moves, sweats, and shivers in step with a plague the God can watch spreading on the map.

### The Faceless in the Ash ✦

*Lens: the Sight and the Books.*

When the Light has visited a dream, the dreamer wakes before dawn and, without thinking, draws in the cold ash of the hearth with one finger: a standing figure, a lantern held up, and no face, only a smooth oval where the face should be. The woman who sweeps the hearth sweeps it away without comment. Over the years, in a valley where many dreams have been sent, the same faceless figure is drawn on hearthstones in different houses by people who have never spoken of it to each other, until one day a child notices, and starts drawing it on purpose, and gives it a name.

- **Driver:** Each Sent Dream leaves one ash drawing in the dreamer's hearth; drawings in different households of the same valley accumulate as a hidden pattern that only the God sees whole, until a curious soul connects them and a myth of the Lantern-Bearer begins.
- **Root:** The First Light walking in dreams with a face never remembered; the Hidden God; myths born from ambiguous signs.
- **Second look:** Children have always drawn in ashes, but these drawings are made by grown people, before dawn, and they are all the same figure.

### The Smoking Grave ✦

*Lens: the body and the dead.*

An Earth-Keeper man could not bear to burn his Ash-Giver wife, so he buried her in his own churchyard. Her grave will not settle. Every frosty morning the mound is warm and steams like a fresh loaf, and on still days a thread of smoke rises from the turned earth as if something beneath it were still smoldering, waiting for the fire it was owed. The flowers he plants there come up gray, the exact gray of ash. He tends the grave every day, and every day it smells of woodsmoke.

- **Driver:** A death given grief but the wrong rite rests uneasily; the grave's warmth and smoke scale with the mourner's grief, and they ease to a gentle warmth if any part of her own rite is honored (her hearth's ash scattered on the mound, her name sung at a fire).
- **Root:** The law that grief lays the dead to rest and the right rite counts more; marriage across Kindreds; uneasy rest.
- **Second look:** Steam off fresh earth on a cold morning is ordinary, but this grave is three years old, and nothing else in the churchyard is steaming.

### The Clock That Keeps the City's Heart ✦

*Lens: the life of the living.*

The first great clock of the soot city was built by the guild with every art it had, and it has never kept true time. In plague years its hours drag so slowly that the noon bell rings at what the sun calls two; in the year of the victory feast it ran so fast that dawn came twice by its count. The clockmakers have rebuilt the escapement four times. They keep a ledger of its errors, year by year, in careful columns, and without knowing it they have written the only honest chronicle of how the city felt.

- **Driver:** A public clock in a city runs fast or slow in proportion to the mean despair or joy of the souls who can hear its bell; the guild's error ledger is a real in-world document the God can read.
- **Root:** The climb toward Advanced Gothic; the fever of Cinerea pressing on time itself; chronicles that are true by accident.
- **Second look:** Early clocks were wildly inaccurate, so a wandering clock is realism, until you lay its error ledger beside the plague years and the lines match.

### The Masks Learn Our Dead ✦

*Lens: the life of the living.*

At the winter carnival of a city that lost many to plague, the masked dancers fill the square under coral lamps, and as the night goes on the masks begin to change. Each one drifts, a line at a time, toward the face of someone a person in the crowd is grieving. A widow sees her husband's long jaw dancing past on a stranger. A boy sees his sister's freckles under a goose-feather brow. The wearers do not know. Some of the bereaved follow the dancers all night, weeping and laughing. Some go home and bar the door.

- **Driver:** A festival with masks in a settlement with high recent grief; each mask drifts toward a face from the grief of the nearest mourner, and how each mourner reacts depends on their temperament and how far along their grief is.
- **Root:** Grief as a force that shapes the world; the dead remembered on the faces of the living; carnival as the old medieval inversion of order.
- **Second look:** Carnival masks are grotesque and strange by nature, but these are slowly becoming portraits, and only the people they belong to recognize them.

### The Same Night at the Way Inn ✦

*Lens: the land and sky.*

Seen from above, it is an ordinary waystation inn on the long road, one lit window, a horse in the yard. But beside the despairing traveler in the corner, the evening repeats. The innkeeper wipes the same cup. The dog scratches the same flea. There is a knock at the door at the same moment. Each time the evening comes round, one thing is missing: first the dog, then the innkeeper's wife, then the fire, until the room is empty and gray and only the knock remains. The traveler looks at the door. If someone speaks to him before the last round, the loop breaks, and it is simply night.

- **Driver:** Only in the Vigil beside a traveler whose despair is high in a place with Tenebrae; the world outside proceeds normally, and companionship (any soul who speaks to him kindly) breaks the loop, while the final knock is the Bargain arriving.
- **Root:** The Nearness Law of the weird (the world drawn as one soul feels it), liminal waystations, and the Bargain heard in one's own despair.
- **Second look:** From the Diorama nothing is wrong at all; only beside him does the God see the room empty itself, one familiar thing at a time.

### The Followers ✦

*Lens: the land and sky.*

In years when despair lies heavy across a whole land, the sun rises with two pale companions, mock suns like those of hard winters, but they do not stay beside it. They trail it across the sky by an hour, one on either side, like children following their mother along a road. Shadows in those years are doubled faintly toward the east. The Ash-Givers call them the Followers and plant late. The Earth-Keepers say nothing, and keep their children indoors at sunset, when the Followers are the last light in the sky.

- **Driver:** Land-wide mean despair above a threshold for a full year produces the Followers; their brightness rises with that despair and they fade in the year it breaks.
- **Root:** Cinerea born sick and feverish: the sky itself shows the fever of the land; each Kindred reads the same sign differently.
- **Second look:** Sun dogs are a real winter sight, but these lag behind the sun by an hour, and they come in summer.

### The Eighth Star ✦

*Lens: the land and sky.*

The Ash-Givers' great constellation, the Plough, has seven stars, and every child of theirs can point to them. The Ledger remembers eight. The eighth went dark in the second century of the long despair, so slowly that no one alive saw it happen, and no living mouth remembers it was ever there. On the Cartograph's inked night sky there is still a tiny pale pinprick where it was, and once in a long while, on the night after a great act of remembrance anywhere in the land, it shows, faint as breath, for an hour.

- **Driver:** A constellation dims star by star across centuries of land-wide despair and forgetting; a star returns briefly on nights after major mourning, Requiem, or a saint's revival, and permanently only if a whole age turns toward remembrance.
- **Root:** Remembrance as light; the Long Sleep and the God's knowledge of what mortals have forgotten; the Ledger against the Chronicles.
- **Second look:** Seven stars in the Plough is what everyone knows, and only the God, comparing the sky to the Ledger, sees the empty place.

### The Road to Hollin ✦

*Lens: the land and sky.*

The old milestones on the east road count down to Hollin: four leagues, three, two, one. The stones are freshly cut each spring by the families along the road, who do it every year because their grandparents did. At the last milestone there is a meadow, a well with no rope, and a smooth green rise where nothing has been built in two hundred years. Carters who travel the road in years of high despair swear they pass the stone marked 'three leagues' twice in one afternoon.

- **Driver:** A town destroyed and never mourned leaves a road custom (recutting the stones) as authored wrongness; in high-Heat years a despairing traveler on that road perceives the repeating milestone in the Vigil, and a Requiem or rite at the meadow ends both the repetition and, a generation later, the custom.
- **Root:** Customs born from forgotten events (a road kept for a town no one remembers), Limen's repetition, and the Ledger that knows where Hollin was.
- **Second look:** Milestones are the most ordinary thing on a road, until you follow them to the end and there is nothing there.

## III. The Scary

*Despair pushes the horror forward.* (12 concepts)

### The Lamps Coming Home

*Lens: the land and sky.* *Absorbs: The Raft Against the Current.*

When a River-Sender raft snags in the reed marsh before it reaches the sea, its lamp does not go out: it sinks and keeps burning under the black water as a dim smudge of coral. As the family forgets, the smudge starts to travel upstream against the current, a few rods each night, under the ice, past the mill weir, through the drowned water meadows, and the fishermen's nets come up warm. One late-winter night it is under the village well, and the bucket comes up brimming with water that glows faintly in the dark and smells of lamp oil and river mud.

- **Driver:** When a river-sending fails and the family's remembrance of that dead person falls, the lamp starts moving upstream, faster as that remembrance falls further and household despair rises, and it stops or drifts back toward the sea when the family renews the rite.
- **Root:** River-Sender lamp rafts, the Shore where a lantern's brightness is remembrance, and the cost of a rite left unfinished.
- **Second look:** A coral glow on marsh water at night passes for marsh-fire or a fisherman's lantern, but this one is underwater, moving against the current and heading for one particular house.

### The Unfed Hill

*Lens: the land and sky.*

When a Crow-Giver is laid out on the hill and nobody grieves, the crows come by the hundred and do not eat. They stand shoulder to shoulder in a ring around the body, every head turned outward toward the village, utterly silent, while the corpse rots untouched beneath them in the rain. A week later a second ring stands on an empty hill nearer in, then a third at the edge of the common, closing like a hand, and whenever a door opens in the village the whole black circle shifts its feathers at once with a sound like a wet sheet being shaken out.

- **Driver:** A Crow-Giver exposure with too little grief starts the first ring, each new ring forms one step nearer the household that owed the mourning, and the birds break the circle and feed the moment someone truly weeps.
- **Root:** Crow-Giver sky burial, the law that grief lays the dead to rest, and the drift of the Unmourned toward the living.
- **Second look:** Crows on a hill above a body are a daily sight in Crow-Giver country, but these do not feed, do not call and all face the same house.

### The Lying-Down Ash

*Lens: the land and sky.*

In the Ashen, gray ash sometimes falls mixed with the snow, and where the unmourned lie thick it settles into the shapes of people lying down. By morning the roofs, the common, the road and the frozen millpond are printed with hollows the length of a man or a child, each in the posture its person died in: curled, sprawled, one arm stretched toward a door. The village walks out among them without speaking, a boy steps carefully around one exactly his size, and the only sound is the soft hiss of ash still falling into the hollows and never filling them.

- **Driver:** When ashfall crosses a region with unmourned dead, it lays one hollow for each of them, at the place and in the posture of their death, and the fall grows heavier as the living despair.
- **Root:** Cinerea the Ashen, the Tenebrae as the weight of unmourned death, and the Earth-Keepers' insistence that the dead be laid down properly.
- **Second look:** Wind-scooped hollows in snow are natural and everywhere, but on a second look you count them, the count is exactly the number of the unmourned, and no footprints lead into or out of any of them.

### The Hands Behind the Surgeon's Tent

*Lens: the body and the dead.*

After a battle the barber-surgeons work all night, and the sawn-off arms and legs pile up in the ditch behind the tent, pale in the mud, unwashed and unnamed. By morning the hands in the heap have found each other and clasp with their fingers laced, in a ring around the pile, like mourners at a grave that no one will ever dig. Inside the tent, the men they were cut from wake gripping the air with hands they no longer have, and swear that someone held them all night. On nights when despair runs through the camp, the heap has moved a little closer to the cots, with drag marks in the mud, and the men scream that their missing hands are being squeezed until the bones grind.

- **Driver:** Battle casualties with amputations whose severed limbs are thrown away without any rite, made worse by low morale and despair in the camp; when someone buries the limbs with each man's Kindred rite, the hands let go and the phantom grip stops.
- **Root:** It applies the law that unmourned death pools in the land to the small deaths no one counts: the severed parts of the living have no mourner, so they mourn each other.
- **Second look:** Heaps of amputated limbs behind field hospitals are a documented historical horror and phantom-limb pain is real medicine, so the scene reads as war realism until the eye catches the laced fingers and the ring.

### One Bowl Short

*Lens: the life of the living.* *Absorbs: The Ledger in Her Own Hand.*

Every morning of the famine winter, a mother wakes before her family and finds the table already set: the wooden bowls laid out in her own neat way, the spoons turned the way she turns them, the bread cut in her own thick slices. There are five bowls and six people in the house, and the missing place is always the youngest's, with the smallest stool pushed back against the wall and the dust swept carefully around its feet. She does not remember rising in the night, but her hands smell of the bread knife, and chalked on the flour bin in her own handwriting are the words: there will be enough.

- **Driver:** A Bargain offer is pending against a despairing mortal, with 'what it wants' and 'what it loves' drawn from simulation state; the household rearranges itself each night toward that outcome, escalates as the mortal's despair rises, and returns to six bowls on the morning the mortal refuses.
- **Root:** It grows from the Bargain, in which despair hears its own voice, in its own handwriting, offering what it wants for what it loves.
- **Second look:** A set table in a peasant kitchen is about as ordinary as an image can be, and only counting the bowls reveals the horror, which most players will do a few mornings too late.

### The Night Shift

*Lens: the life of the living.*

Around the half-built mill of the soot town the scaffolding climbs in a black lattice, and after the evening bell, when the living crews go home, figures stand on the planks in the attitudes of work: one bent under a hod of bricks, one reaching for a rope, one leaning out over nothing with an arm flung up. They do not move all night, and each one stands on the exact plank where a man fell, was carried off, and went into a common pit with no name and no rite over him. In the morning the living climb past them and through them, and the mortar laid in those spots never quite sets. Every year the building rises a little higher, and every year there are more of them.

- **Driver:** Construction deaths on a building that went unmourned, such as laborers far from kin who were buried without rite, persist as frozen silhouettes at the places they fell, multiply with each new death, and dissolve one by one when someone names and grieves them.
- **Root:** It grows from the Unmourned, griefs with bodies, rising out of the slow climb toward Advanced Gothic, with its scaffolding, soot and first engines.
- **Second look:** From the diorama camera at night they read as tarps, tools and shadows on the scaffold, until you notice their postures are human and their number matches the building's death count.

### The Downward Prayer

*Lens: the life of the living.*

After midnight the prayers of the valley rise from the roofs as threads of faint rose light, hundreds of them, swaying like river grass in a slow current. One thread runs the other way: it comes down out of the starless dark, taut as a fishing line and the color of cold ash, and enters the chimney of the cooper's house, humming at a pitch you feel in your back teeth. His hearth still burns among the Lights of the Living, still coral, still small and homely, but his chimney smoke pours downward and spreads around the house like spilled water, pooling toward you across the yard. Frost lies thick and unmelted on the roof above the fire.

- **Driver:** When a mortal becomes Unlit by accepting the Bargain, their nightly devotion is drawn as an inverted prayer thread, and their hearth stays among the Lights of the Living but gives off no heat.
- **Root:** It grows from prayers rising at night as threads of light, from the silent Void that mortal will gives a voice, and from the Unlit who accepted the Bargain.
- **Second look:** It is one more line among hundreds and one more lit window among the Lights of the Living, but its thread runs the wrong way, its color is ash, and its smoke flows down instead of up.

### The Chronicle Copies Itself

*Lens: the Sight and the Books.*

During the Long Sleep the Chronicle turns one century to a breath, with a sound like wind moving through dry barley. When a valley slides into the same famine and the same unmourned winter it suffered three hundred years before, the new page comes out as a perfect copy of the old one: the same miniature of the same Crow-Giver hill with the same dead laid out under the same empty, birdless sky, the same sentence about the failed harvest, only the ink still wet. Then the mothers in that valley, without knowing why, begin naming their newborns after the old page's unmourned dead, and the Book of the Living writes those names in a scribe's hand that stopped writing three centuries ago.

- **Driver:** Triggers when a region's present conditions (famine, unmourned count, skipped rites) closely match a past Chronicle entry for the same place; the closeness of the match sets how exact the copy is, and the naming echo starts only if that old entry's dead were never laid to rest.
- **Root:** Grows from TESTIMONIUM, where land heavy with grief replays its memory, and from the Unmourned who were never laid to rest: unresolved dead come back first as a pattern and then as names.
- **Second look:** Chronicles copying older chronicles and children named after long-dead ancestors are completely ordinary medieval practice, but here the copy is exact down to the empty sky, and the names belong to the dead nobody mourned.

### The Printer's Devil ✦

*Lens: the life of the living.*

In the first print shop of the soot town, the apprentice opens the shutters each morning and finds a line of type already set in the composing stick that nobody set: a list of names, the people who burned in the dye-works fire and went into the pit unmourned. Each night the list is longer. When the master angrily prints it to prove it is a prank, the ink will not dry on the names; it stays wet for weeks, and comes off on the fingers of anyone who touches the sheet. The day the town reads the names aloud in the square, the type is found sorted back into its cases.

- **Driver:** Unmourned deaths in a settlement that has a printing press; the list grows with the unmourned count, and resolves when a public reading of the names counts as mourning for each.
- **Root:** The Tenebrae, the hand that reveals the author (the stamped hand of the Ledger), and the printing press that could make the dead impossible to forget.
- **Second look:** Apprentices misset type every day, which is why printers call them devils, but no apprentice knew these names.

### The Lord's Clean Hall ✦

*Lens: the life of the living.*

The lord who put a village to the sword keeps his hall immaculate: swept flags, fresh rushes, the great fresco of the hunt behind his chair repainted in bright colors. But in the fresco's far background, between the painted trees, small figures have begun to appear, one for each of the dead, each in the posture it died in, painted in a hand the court painter swears is not his. Guests at the feast never notice them. The lord does. He has the wall whitewashed and painted again. Within a season the first figure is back, smaller, nearer the front.

- **Driver:** Deliberate atrocity whose victims went unmourned; the figures appear at the rate the perpetrator's guilt (Ashes) grows, nearer the foreground as his despair rises, and they stop only if he confesses and the dead receive their rites.
- **Root:** Authored wrongness inside a painting; the Tenebrae born of cruelty rather than neglect; guilt as Ashes in the soul of the guilty.
- **Second look:** Hunting frescoes are crowded with small background figures, but these are not hunters, and they are not running.

### The Pass of Two Footprints ✦

*Lens: the land and sky.*

On the high pass where a refugee column froze in the year of the long winter, every new snow shows two sets of footprints for every traveler: their own, and a second, smaller set walking close beside them. Where the dead of the pass were mourned, the second prints keep pace at the traveler's shoulder all the way down, and the travelers arrive saying they were not alone. Where they were not, the second prints walk a step ahead, and at the place where the path bends above the drop, they keep going straight.

- **Driver:** Mountain-pass deaths; the companion prints exist wherever the pass's dead exceed a threshold, and their behavior is set by whether those dead were mourned (walking beside, or leading away), with a lost traveler's despair making them harder to resist.
- **Root:** The Unmourned drifting toward the living, and the mourned dead as quiet companions; the same phenomenon in two tones.
- **Second look:** Tracks in snow on a pass are ordinary, but no one ever walked beside these travelers, and the second prints are a child's size.

### The Night the Roads Fill ✦

*Lens: the land and sky.*

When the Strepitus comes, the sky at midday turns the color of a bruise and the birds walk instead of flying. That night, every road in the region fills with people walking: in the clothes of every century, Earth-Keeper wool and Ash-Giver leather of three hundred years ago, soldiers in the old harness of the marsh battle, children in shifts. They walk in perfect silence toward the places where they died, carrying nothing, not looking up. The living shutter their windows. In one cottage an old woman opens her door, sets a heel of bread on the step, and says a name, and one of the walkers stops.

- **Driver:** The Strepitus threshold; every Unmourned of the region walks; each household that performs a rite or speaks a name for one of them that night lays that one to rest and removes them from the road.
- **Root:** The Strepitus, the Unmourned, and the law that mercy is the only thing that truly pushes back the dark.
- **Second look:** There is nothing subtle here, except that in the whole procession there is not one sound, and the one walker who stops is the only one who turns his head.

## Held in reserve

Alternates the judge set aside for overlap, kept for later: **The Fond Map** (the Cartograph draws dead villages with growing tenderness, labeled in the last inhabitant's hand), **The Rag-Seller's Line** and **The Dead-Cart Compass** (the dead pointing toward what they are owed), **The Sleeper in the Barley** (a cropmark sleeper curling tighter each year), **The Kept Holding** (a dead family's farm graying but never decaying).

Rejected for cause: **The Undercloud Hearth** (a region-wide glow answering prayer would be public proof of God), **The Tolls Past the Count** (the self-ringing bell is stock ghost-story imagery), **The Square Holds Its Breath** (driven by the camera rather than by Cinerea's grief or faith).
